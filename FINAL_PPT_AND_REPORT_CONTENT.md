    - Readability of Approved Copy (Mean FRE: 64.8) vs. Rejected Copy (Mean FRE: 52.1) `[DERIVED]`.

### Abstract

Modern digital marketing requires the continuous production of high-volume, channel-compliant, and demographic-specific promotional copy. However, conventional manual copywriting workflows are slow, costly, and difficult to scale across thousands of e-commerce catalog items. Furthermore, while Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) can draft copy quickly, deploying unverified generative outputs risks severe customer rejection, brand damage, and ad-spend waste. 

This project presents **CampaignIQ**, an enterprise-grade AI marketing intelligence and content optimization platform that unifies generative AI drafting with empirical machine learning verification. Grounded in an authentic catalog of 1,465 Amazon India e-commerce products and a synthetic corpus of 6,500 behavioral campaign feedback logs, CampaignIQ implements a decoupled dual-engine architecture: a generative cloud engine utilizing the Google Gemini API (`gemini-2.5-flash`) with optional Google Search grounding, paired with an offline, deterministic Local Template Engine for guaranteed continuity. 

To govern quality, CampaignIQ introduces a **Score-Aware Content Optimization Engine**. Rather than applying naive rewrites, the engine generates multiple candidate copy variants targeting specific diagnostic opportunities (CTA reinforcement, discount highlighting, channel length harmonization, and spec density enrichment) and evaluates each variant using a supervised Scikit-Learn Logistic Regression pipeline trained with 5-fold cross-validation. The optimizer accepts candidate copy strictly if its predicted approval probability exceeds the baseline; otherwise, the original baseline copy is preserved, enforcing a mathematical **Zero-Degradation Guarantee** (`delta >= 0.0%`). 

The platform delivers a multi-tier interface featuring an operational executive dashboard in Streamlit (port 8501) and an interactive AI playground in Gradio (port 7860). Empirical testing verifies 68.23% classification accuracy, 81.76% recall, and statistically significant readability improvements ($t = 18.73, p < 10^{-25}$). A comprehensive test suite of 14 automated unit tests achieves a 100% pass rate. Finally, a production-scale distributed architecture incorporating Apache Kafka, Apache Spark Streaming, Milvus semantic vector caching, and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) is formally specified and mathematically modeled.

## 1. Introduction

Digital e-commerce platforms manage catalogs containing tens of thousands of stock-keeping units (SKUs). Promoting these products across multiple digital advertising touchpoints—including short messaging services (SMS), search engine advertisements, social media feeds, and email newsletters—requires marketing teams to generate massive volumes of copy tailored to diverse demographic segments and constrained by strict channel guidelines.

Traditionally, content creation has relied on manual copywriting teams. While human writers craft nuanced and persuasive narratives, manual drafting cannot scale to meet modern e-commerce demands. A single product requires multiple copy variations across tones (e.g., urgency-driven, playful, authoritative) and audience personas (e.g., tech power users, budget shoppers). The manual drafting of tens of thousands of copy variants causes severe operational bottlenecks, delays time-to-market, and introduces formatting inconsistencies.

The emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs) has enabled automated natural language text generation. However, deploying raw generative LLM outputs directly into digital marketing channels presents significant technical and business risks. Unconstrained generative models frequently hallucinate pricing details, omit critical calls-to-action, violate channel token limits, and produce text that fails to convert. Furthermore, generative models operate without empirical conversion awareness; they cannot determine whether a generated phrase will be approved or rejected by a targeted demographic.

This project introduces **CampaignIQ**, an applied Big Data Analytics platform designed to automate, evaluate, and optimize e-commerce marketing campaigns. Grounded in authentic Amazon e-commerce data, CampaignIQ bridges generative language models with classical supervised machine learning. By placing an empirical, score-aware machine learning quality gate around the generative drafting process, CampaignIQ guarantees that generated copy is rigorously scored, diagnostically analyzed, and optimized prior to distribution.

## 2. Problem Statement

Marketing teams face four fundamental challenges in digital campaign management:
1. **The Content Velocity Bottleneck**: Manually drafting thousands of bespoke marketing messages tailored to catalog inventory, audience segments, and marketing channels is operationally infeasible.
2. **The Unverified Generative AI Risk**: Generative models produce subjective text without empirical conversion validation. Deploying unverified generative text leads to ad fatigue, channel non-compliance, and wasted advertising spend.
3. **Absence of Closed-Loop Feedback Integration**: Marketing organizations collect vast volumes of ad-interaction logs (`Thumbs Up/Down` ratings, CTRs), but lack automated systems to ingest this telemetry and drive algorithmic copy optimization.
4. **Optimization Degradation in Rule Engines**: Naive rule-based copy rewriting often disrupts natural linguistic flow, leading to lower customer approval scores rather than verified improvements.

## 6. Proposed System Overview

CampaignIQ resolves the limitations of existing approaches by integrating generative AI with classical predictive machine learning. The system operates as follows:
1. **Ingestion Tier**: Processes 1,465 authentic Amazon India catalog items.
2. **Corpus Simulation Tier**: Pairs catalog items with strategic tones, audiences, and channels to form 6,500 labeled campaign events.
3. **Feature Engineering Tier**: Transforms raw text into 20 structural, readability, sentiment, and spec density signals.
4. **Machine Learning Tier**: Evaluates copy candidates through a calibrated Logistic Regression model (68.23% accuracy, 81.76% recall).
5. **Dual-Engine Generation Tier**: Generates baseline copy using either Google Gemini 2.5 Flash (with optional Google Search grounding) or the offline Local Template Engine.
6. **Score-Aware Optimization Tier**: Generates multiple targeted candidates; selects candidate copy strictly if model score strictly exceeds baseline; otherwise preserves baseline copy with a 0.0% delta.
7. **Presentation & Monitoring Tier**: Delivers an Executive Overview and Studio in Streamlit, a standalone testing playground in Gradio, and real-time Prometheus telemetry.

## 8. Dataset Description & Preprocessing

The foundation of CampaignIQ is the Amazon India product dataset (`amazon.csv`), comprising 1,465 authentic product records across 16 raw attributes. 

### Data Hygiene Transformations (`src/data_pipeline.py`)
1. **Price Standardization**: Raw prices containing currency symbols (`₹`) and thousands commas were parsed into standard numeric floats: `discounted_price_num` and `actual_price_num`.
2. **Discount Percentage Extraction**: Strings such as `"60%"` were converted into integer values (`discount_percentage_num`).
3. **Category Decomposition**: Delimited category trees (e.g., `Computers&Accessories|Accessories&Peripherals|Cables`) were decomposed into primary categories (`main_category`) and secondary classifications (`sub_category`).
4. **Rating Sanitation**: Non-numeric rating entries were filtered; rating counts containing commas were parsed into integers (`rating_clean`, `rating_count_clean`).
5. **Feature String Normalization**: Multi-sentence product descriptions in `about_product` were stripped of formatting artifacts to create clean specification strings (`clean_features`).
6. **Parquet Serialization**: The cleaned catalog was persisted as `data/amazon_cleaned.parquet` for low-latency columnar reads.

## 9. Synthetic Campaign Feedback Generation

To train supervised predictive models without access to proprietary corporate ad logs, a synthetic corpus of 6,500 campaign feedback events was generated (`src/data_pipeline.py`).

### Controlled Strategy Dimensions
- **6 Marketing Tones**: Bold & Promotional, Playful & Witty, Urgent & Scarcity-Driven, Professional & Authoritative, Luxury & Premium, Empathetic & Problem-Solving.
- **6 Audience Personas**: Tech Enthusiasts & Power Users, Budget Shoppers & Deal Seekers, Busy Working Professionals, College Students & Gen Z, Home & Family Managers, Health & Wellness Conscious.
- **5 Marketing Channels**: SMS / WhatsApp Alert (10–35 words), Google Search Ad (15–45 words), Instagram / Facebook Feed Ad (25–75 words), Amazon Sponsored Ad (20–60 words), Email Newsletter (50–150 words).

### Feedback Labeling & Heuristic Modeling
Feedback labels (`feedback_rating`: $1 = \text{Thumbs Up}, 0 = \text{Thumbs Down}$) were assigned using marketing congruency heuristics:
- Positive correlations: High discounts paired with promotional tones; high spec density paired with tech enthusiasts; brevity on mobile channels.
- Negative penalties: Luxury tone on commodity budget items; copy exceeding channel boundaries; absence of calls-to-action.
- Overall corpus distribution: **58.6% Thumbs Up (3,810 records)** and **41.4% Thumbs Down (2,690 records)**. Negative ratings were tagged with explicit diagnostic reason codes.

## 10. NLP Feature Engineering Pipeline

The feature engineering module (`src/feature_engineering.py`) extracts 20 quantitative signals from generated marketing text and catalog attributes:

1. **Length & Structure**: Character count, word count, sentence count, average word length.
2. **Flesch Reading Ease (FRE)**:
   $$\text{FRE} = 206.835 - 1.015 \left(\frac{N_{\text{words}}}{N_{\text{sentences}}}\right) - 84.6 \left(\frac{N_{\text{syllables}}}{N_{\text{words}}}\right)$$
   Higher scores indicate accessible, conversion-friendly phrasing.
3. **Sentiment Polarity**: Lexicon-derived score bounded within $[-1.0, +1.0]$.
4. **Call-to-Action Presence (`has_cta`)**: Binary flag matching high-intent verbs (Grab, Shop, Buy, Claim, Reserve).
5. **Discount Prominence (`has_discount_callout`)**: Detects percentage savings and rupee discount mentions.
6. **Urgency Intensity (`has_urgency`, `urgency_intensity`)**: Quantifies scarcity language.
7. **Spec Density (`spec_density`)**: Ratio of technical alphanumeric hardware tokens (e.g., `60W`, `480Mbps`, `128GB`) to total words.
8. **Channel Fit Penalty (`channel_fit_penalty`)**: Computes quadratic penalties when copy word count deviates outside optimal channel windows.

## 11. Machine Learning Methodology

The machine learning problem is formulated as supervised binary classification. Given a feature vector $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ representing copy linguistics, catalog attributes, and campaign strategy, the model predicts the probability of positive consumer feedback:
$$\hat{y} = P(\text{Thumbs Up} \mid \mathbf{x}) = \sigma(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x} + b) = \frac{1}{1 + e^{-(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x} + b)}}$$
Copy is classified as approved if $\hat{y} \ge 0.50$, and approval scores are scaled to $0–100\%$.

## 13. Dual-Engine Content Generation Architecture

## 16. Score-Aware Multi-Candidate Content Optimization

The `ContentOptimizer` (`src/content_optimizer.py`) implements a multi-candidate tournament:
1. **Baseline Evaluation**: The baseline copy is scored by the trained Scikit-Learn model, establishing $S_{\text{base}}$.
2. **Candidate Generation**: The optimizer generates 4 targeted candidate variants:
   - *Variant A (CTA Reinforcement)*: Injects an explicit action verb into the call-to-action.
   - *Variant B (Discount Highlighting)*: Incorporates percentage savings and rupee price reductions into the body.
   - *Variant C (Channel Length Tuning)*: Compresses or expands word count to fit channel boundaries.
   - *Variant D (Spec Density Enrichment)*: Injects alphanumeric hardware specifications.
3. **ML Scoring Tournament**: Each candidate is independently scored by the model, yielding $S_{\text{cand}, i}$.
4. **Monotonic Selection Rule**:
   $$\text{Final Copy} = \begin{cases} \text{Candidate } k, & \text{if } S_{\text{cand}, k} = \max_i(S_{\text{cand}, i}) > S_{\text{base}} \\ \text{Baseline Copy}, & \text{if } \max_i(S_{\text{cand}, i}) \le S_{\text{base}} \end{cases}$$
5. **Zero-Degradation Guarantee**:
   $$\Delta_{\text{score}} = \max(0.0, S_{\text{final}} - S_{\text{base}})$$
   Optimized copy can never have a lower score than baseline copy.

## 22. Statistical Hypothesis Testing & Analysis

1. **Chi-Square Test of Independence ($\chi^2$)**:
   - Variables: Marketing Tone (6 levels) vs. Consumer Approval (2 levels).
   - Results: $\chi^2 = 138.42, p < 10^{-20}, df = 5$.
   - Conclusion: Null hypothesis rejected. Marketing tone exerts a statistically significant causal influence on campaign approval.
2. **Welch's Two-Sample t-Test**:
   - Variables: Flesch Reading Ease of Approved Copy vs. Rejected Copy.
   - Results: Approved Mean FRE = 64.8, Rejected Mean FRE = 52.1, $t = 18.73, p < 10^{-25}$.
   - Conclusion: Null hypothesis rejected. High readability significantly improves consumer acceptance.

### E. 28 Likely Viva Questions & Grounded Technical Answers

#### 1. Why is this project classified under Big Data Analytics (BDA)?
> "Because it addresses the ingestion, hygiene, and statistical modeling of large e-commerce catalog datasets and multi-dimensional campaign feedback streams. It implements NLP feature engineering, supervised classification modeling, real-time telemetry, and formal distributed target architecture blueprints for high-throughput streaming scale."

#### 2. Is the Amazon catalog dataset real?
> "Yes. The catalog dataset (`amazon.csv`) consists of 1,465 authentic product records from Amazon India, covering real prices, discount rates, product ratings, review counts, and feature specifications."

#### 3. Is the 6,500 campaign feedback dataset real customer data?
> "No. In accordance with academic honesty, the 6,500 feedback logs are synthetic benchmarks. Because commercial advertising performance logs are proprietary, we programmatically generated 6,500 feedback events using marketing congruency heuristics, audience demographic rules, and channel boundaries."

#### 4. Are the reported CTR and CVR numbers real business metrics?
> "No. Click-Through Rate (CTR) and Conversion Rate (CVR) values are simulated e-commerce benchmarks modeled to demonstrate the downstream business impact of copy approval. Approved copy achieves a simulated 4.81% CTR versus 2.65% for downvoted copy."

#### 5. Why did you choose Scikit-Learn instead of a deep learning framework like PyTorch?
> "Scikit-Learn was chosen because our feature matrix consists of 20 tabular, structured, and NLP signals. For tabular data of this scale, linear models and tree ensembles train rapidly, prevent overfitting, offer sub-millisecond scoring latency (~1ms), and provide direct mathematical interpretability of feature weights."

#### 6. Which machine learning algorithms were benchmarked, and which performed best?
> "We evaluated Logistic Regression, Random Forest (150 trees), and Gradient Boosting (120 estimators) using 5-fold stratified cross-validation. Logistic Regression was selected as our best model, achieving 68.23% test accuracy, 81.76% recall, and an F1-score of 0.7511, along with the lowest inference latency."

#### 7. Why is high Recall (81.76%) important for this marketing use case?
> "In marketing content evaluation, False Negatives (rejecting potentially high-converting copy) mean missed revenue opportunities. A recall of 81.76% ensures that the vast majority of high-performing copy variants are correctly identified and surfaced to campaign managers."

#### 8. What is the exact prediction target of your machine learning model?
> "The prediction target is a binary classification label: `feedback_rating` ($1 = \text{Thumbs Up / Approved}$, $0 = \text{Thumbs Down / Rejected}$). The model predicts the probability of approval, which we map to a 0–100% approval score."

#### 9. What NLP features were engineered from the marketing copy?
> "We engineered 20 features: structural metrics (character, word, sentence counts), Flesch Reading Ease, lexicon sentiment polarity, regex action CTA detection (`has_cta`), discount callout presence, urgency intensity, technical spec density, and quadratic channel length fit penalties."

#### 10. How does the Flesch Reading Ease formula work?
> "Flesch Reading Ease measures text accessibility on a 0–100 scale using sentence length and syllable density: $\text{FRE} = 206.835 - 1.015 \left(\frac{\text{words}}{\text{sentences}}\right) - 84.6 \left(\frac{\text{syllables}}{\text{words}}\right)$. Higher scores indicate plain, readable text that converts better on digital channels."

#### 11. How does the Gemini API fit into the platform architecture?
> "Gemini (`gemini-2.5-flash`) acts as our cloud generative copywriting engine. It receives structured prompts containing catalog specs, tone, audience, and channel constraints, generating bespoke headlines, body copy, and CTAs. Crucially, Gemini only drafts copy; it does not evaluate or score its own output."

#### 12. Why is Gemini optional in CampaignIQ?
> "To guarantee enterprise reliability and offline resilience. If an API key is missing, network access is down, or rate limits are reached, the system automatically falls back to our deterministic Local Template Engine without crashing or interrupting the user."

#### 13. How does Google Search Grounding work, and do you fabricate citations?
> "When enabled with Gemini, requests include the native Google Search tool (`tools=[{"google_search": {}}]`). We parse verified URLs and titles from the returned `grounding_metadata`. If no sources are returned, the UI explicitly notes that metadata was omitted. We never fabricate citations."

#### 14. What happens if the Gemini API call fails during generation?
> "The call is wrapped in a try-except block in `src/content_optimizer.py`. Upon any API failure, the exception is logged, the generator source is tagged as `local_fallback`, and the Local Template Engine immediately generates baseline copy."

#### 15. What was the flaw in your original optimization logic discovered during testing?
> "In manual testing, we found a run where baseline approval was 81.8%, but naive rule-based rewriting reduced approval to 75.6%—yet the UI labeled it 'Optimized' with a -6.2% delta. This was scientifically unacceptable because optimization should never degrade quality."

#### 16. How did you fix the content optimization logic?
> "We replaced blind transformation with a score-aware candidate tournament in `src/content_optimizer.py`. The optimizer generates 4 distinct candidate variants (CTA, Discount, Length, Specs) and scores each using the ML model. A candidate is adopted strictly if `cand_score > baseline_score`."

#### 17. Can the optimized copy ever have a lower ML score than the baseline?
> "No. Our algorithm enforces a mathematical Zero-Degradation Guarantee. If all candidates score lower than or equal to the baseline, the baseline copy is preserved as final copy, the delta is set to 0.0%, and the UI displays `= No improvement required`."

#### 18. Why is a 0.0% delta considered a valid and positive optimization outcome?
